Scope & Prioritization

Turning an undefined migration into a structured roadmap

Once the approach was decided, nobody knew how big the problem actually was. There was no map of the existing application, no estimate of the work, and no agreed starting point. Without that foundation, every prioritization conversation was just opinion.

When I audited the entire application myself — page by page, workflow by workflow, functionality by functionality — the migration turned out to be 2–3x larger than anyone had expected.

I was the only person who could create that ground truth, so I did.

total estimated pages: X

If completing X pages per week, need Y weeks to complete the entire product, that's Z months (A years)

For workspace + dashboard, by the speed defined above, it will require B weeks to complete (C months)

Complete means design is finalized (which includes iterations and user testings)

Information architecture estimates — sitemap comparisons across the existing application that surfaced the true scope.

I came into the triad discussion with a clear point of view: start with navigation first, because it touches every workflow downstream and the user journey has to begin somewhere logical.

PM brought the customer value lens. Engineering brought feasibility and spotted that some workflows shared screens and could be simplified rather than just migrated — a valuable challenge I incorporated. I brought the user journey lens.

The roadmap became a genuine triad decision, but it was built on the foundation I created and sequenced around the logic I proposed.

The audit gave the team its first honest look at what they were actually taking on. The roadmap held.

And the reframe — that this was an opportunity to simplify, not just migrate — came directly from having a designer in the room thinking about the user journey, not just the task list.
